What is a good engagement rate on YouTube?

A video reached a million views, but almost no comments or likes — is that real success? The answer is more complicated than you might expect. Views alone are not a sufficient indicator of how engaged your audience is with your content. In this guide we’ll explain a much more important metric: the engagement rate.

Why is engagement more important than views alone?

Views can come from various sources — some are genuinely targeted audience members interested in your content, and some are casual visitors who discovered your video by chance and never engaged with it. The engagement rate actually measures how many of those who watched the video interacted seriously (like, comment, share), which is a much more accurate indicator of the strength of the relationship between your content and your actual audience, regardless of the total number of views.

How is the engagement rate calculated?

The basic formula is simple: (likes + comments + shares) ÷ views × 100 = engagement rate percentage. For example, a video with 10,000 views, 500 likes, and 80 comments has an engagement rate of about 5.8%, which is considered very strong according to common standards.

Approximate evaluation criteria

  • Less than 1%: Low rate — the audience is not sufficiently engaged with the content.
  • 1% to 3%: An average and natural ratio for most channels.
  • 3% to 6%: A good rate indicates a strong connection with the audience.
  • More than 6%: Excellent rate, often on small channels with a very loyal audience.

These numbers are approximate and vary depending on the channel size and niche — do not consider them an absolute strict standard.

How to increase your audience engagement?

Add a clear invitation to interact within the video itself — ask a specific question that encourages the viewer to write their opinion instead of Public "Click Like & Subscribe." Reply to comments regularly, especially in the early hours after posting — this Encourages broader discussion. Finally, design content that provokes a natural opinion or debate rather than completely neutral content It pushes someone to express their opinion.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a high number of views necessarily mean a high engagement rate?

No, viral videos sometimes reach millions of views from a random, uninterested audience, resulting in a low engagement rate despite the large number.

Is the same interaction rate suitable for all channel sizes?

No, small channels with a loyal audience often achieve higher engagement rates than large channels.

Which factors are usually included in the engagement rate?

It is usually calculated by dividing total likes, comments, and shares by the number of views, then multiplying by 100.

How do I increase audience engagement?

Adding a clear call to interact, regularly responding to comments, and designing content that sparks natural discussion are all practices that increase engagement.
