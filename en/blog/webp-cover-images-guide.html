Why You Should Convert Your Blog Images to WebP Before Publishing

If you run a blog or an online store, your images are probably the single biggest weight on your site's speed. Every JPG or PNG you add increases page size, and a larger page means slower loading — and site speed is a direct factor in both your Google ranking and your visitor's experience.

What is the WebP format?

WebP is an image format developed by Google specifically for the web. The core idea is simple: roughly the same visual quality you'd get from JPG or PNG, but at a noticeably smaller file size — typically 25 to 35% smaller than JPG at equivalent visual quality, and considerably smaller than PNG for images that need transparency.

Why does this difference actually matter?

Imagine an article with 10 images, each 200 kilobytes as a JPG. That's 2 extra megabytes a visitor's browser has to load just for the images. If you convert those same images to WebP at roughly the same quality, that could drop to under 1.5 megabytes — a real saving that shows up in page load speed, especially on mobile and slower internet connections.

Google itself recommends using modern image formats like WebP as part of the Core Web Vitals standards that influence a site's ranking in search results.

When to use WebP, and when not to

WebP is suitable for nearly every web use case: article images, product photos, thumbnails, and even cover images. The only exception is if you need compatibility with extremely old browsers that are barely used anymore today, or if you need to keep an original image in its native format for printing or future editing — in that case, keep an original copy and publish a WebP version only.

WebP's impact on search engine optimization

SEO depends on dozens of factors, but site speed is one of the most important and directly measurable of them all. Tools like Google PageSpeed Insights explicitly recommend using modern image formats like WebP as a first step toward improving your performance score. Beyond its impact on search ranking, a faster page also reduces early visitor drop-off (bounce rate), which is another indirect signal that benefits your ranking over the long run.

Frequently asked questions

Do all modern browsers support the WebP format?

Yes, every major modern browser has fully supported WebP for years, and global usage coverage now exceeds 95% of users.

Does WebP actually affect my ranking in Google search results?

Indirectly, yes — Google treats page load speed as an official ranking factor within Core Web Vitals, and WebP directly contributes to improving that metric.

Do I lose image quality when converting to WebP?

No noticeable visual difference at reasonable quality settings (80% and above) — WebP delivers roughly the same quality at a much smaller file size.

Should I keep an original JPG or PNG copy alongside the WebP version?

It's good practice to keep an original in your personal archive, but for publishing on the site itself, a WebP version alone is entirely sufficient for most cases.

Conclusion

Converting your images to WebP is a simple step that takes minutes, but its impact on your site's speed, search ranking, and visitor experience is real and measurable. If you're still publishing JPG or PNG images without converting them, this is the easiest optimization you can make today.
