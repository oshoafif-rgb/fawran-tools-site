Why You Should Convert Your Blog Images to WebP

If you run a blog or an online store, your images are probably the biggest weight dragging down your page speed. Every JPG or PNG you add increases the page size, and a bigger page means slower loading — and site speed is a direct ranking factor on Google, plus a big part of the visitor's experience.

What is WebP?

WebP is an image format developed by Google specifically for the web. The core idea is simple: roughly the same visual quality you'd get from JPG or PNG, but at a noticeably smaller file size — often 25 to 35% smaller than JPG at equivalent visual quality.

Why does this actually matter?

Imagine an article with 10 images, each 200KB as JPG. That's 2MB extra a visitor's browser has to load just for images. Convert those same images to WebP at similar quality, and you could drop under 1.5MB — a real saving that shows up directly in page load speed, especially on mobile and slower connections.

Rule of thumb: quality between 70% and 85% gives an excellent balance between size and visual quality for most images — you rarely need 100% quality for ordinary web images.

When should you use WebP, and when not?

WebP is suitable for nearly every use case on the web: article images, product photos, thumbnails, even cover images. The one exception is if you need compatibility with very old browsers that are barely used today, or if you need to keep the original file format for printing or later editing — in that case, keep an original copy and publish a WebP version only.
