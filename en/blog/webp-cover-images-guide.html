Why you should convert your blog images to WebP before publishing

If you run a blog or an e-commerce store, your images are often the biggest load on your site speed. Every JPG or PNG image you add increases page size, and a larger page means slower loading — and site speed is a direct factor in your Google ranking and visitor experience.

What is the WebP format?

WebP is an image format developed by Google specifically for the web. The main idea is simple: almost the same visual quality you get from JPG or PNG, but with a significantly smaller file size — often 25 to 35% smaller than JPG at the same visual quality, and much smaller than PNG for images with transparency.

Why is this difference actually important?

Imagine an article with 10 images, each 200 KB as a JPG. This means an additional 2 MB being loaded by the visitor's browser just for the images. If you convert the same images to WebP at roughly the same quality, it could go down to less than 1.5 MB — a real saving that reflects in page load speed, especially on mobile and slow internet networks.

Google itself recommends using modern image formats like WebP as part of the Core Web Vitals standards that affect a site's ranking in search results.

When to use WebP and when not to?

WebP is suitable for almost all web uses: article images, product images, thumbnails, and even cover photos. The only exception is if you need compatibility with very old browsers that are almost no longer used today, or if you need to keep the original image in its format for printing or later editing — in this case, keep an original copy and publish only the WebP version.

How do you easily convert your images to WebP?

You don’t need a complex photo editing program or technical experience. The easiest way is to upload the images to an online conversion tool that processes them directly in your browser, allowing you to adjust quality and resize at the same time — ideal if you’re posting on a platform like Blogger and need a specific cover size.

The impact of WebP on SEO

Search Engine Optimization (SEO) relies on dozens of factors, but site speed is one of the most important and most directly impactful, and measurable. Tools like Google PageSpeed Insights explicitly recommend using modern image formats like WebP as a first step to improve performance scores. In addition to its effect on search ranking, a faster page reduces early visitor exit rate (Bounce Rate), which is another indirect signal that benefits your ranking over the long term.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do all modern browsers support the WebP format?

Yes, all major modern browsers have fully supported WebP for years, and its global usage exceeds 95% of users.

Does WebP really affect my site's ranking in Google search results?

Indirectly yes — Google considers page load speed as an official ranking factor within Core Web Vitals, and WebP contributes directly to improving this metric.

Do I lose image quality when converting to WebP?

There is no noticeable visual difference when using reasonable quality settings (80% and above) — WebP gives almost the same quality at a much smaller size.

Should I keep an original JPG or PNG version alongside WebP?

It is preferable to keep an original copy in your personal archive, but for publishing on the website itself, a WebP version alone is perfectly sufficient in most cases.

Summary

Converting your images to WebP is a simple step that takes minutes, but its impact on your website speed, search ranking, and visitor experience is real and tangible. If you are still publishing JPG or PNG images without converting, this is the easiest improvement you can make today.
