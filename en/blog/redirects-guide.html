301 or 302? A guide to the correct link redirection for your site

  • When do you need redirection in the first place?

  • How to create redirect rules?
  • Common Errors That Harm SEO
  • Frequently Asked Questions

Changed a link to an important page on your site, or deleted an old article? If no one redirected old visitors to the new location, they will reach a "Not Found" (404) page — which is a poor experience for visitors and a negative signal to Google. Redirecting is the solution, and in this guide we’ll explain how to do it correctly.

When do you need redirection in the first place?

  • Changing Link Structure: I moved an article from one section to another, so its link changed.
  • Delete an old page: A discontinued product or an old article you decided to delete in favor of a more comprehensive one.
  • Merge two pages into one: I combined the content of two similar articles into one stronger page.
  • Change domain: Moved to a new domain and need to redirect all old visitors automatically.

Difference Between 301 and 302

301 (Permanent Redirect): It tells the browser and search engine that the page has permanently moved to the new address. Google transfers most of the “ranking power” from the old page to the new one, which is the key practical difference between it and a 302.

302 (Temporary Redirect): It indicates that the move is temporary only — the original page will return to work soon. Google retains the original page in its index because it expects it to return, and it does not transfer the same ranking strength to the new page. Use it only if the move is truly temporary (maintenance, temporary A/B testing).

Common Errors That Harm SEO

The most common mistake is using 302 to permanently move by mistake — the result is that you lose the power of the old page ranking without Need. Second error: long redirect chains (page A with a Lb, B with a C) instead of a direct redirect from A lj — long strings slow down page loading and sometimes confuse search engines. Always direct From the old link to the final link without intermediate terminals.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should I use a 301 redirect instead of a 302?

Use 301 when the transfer is permanent, and use 302 when the transfer is temporary only.

Does a redirect convey the SEO power of the new page?

A 301 redirect transfers most of the ranking power, whereas a 302 does not transfer it as efficiently because it indicates a temporary move.

What is the difference between a .htaccess file and Nginx settings?

.htaccess is specific to Apache servers, while Nginx uses a completely different configuration format within its own configuration files.

Can multiple old links be redirected all at once?

Yes, multiple pairs of old and new links can be added and all redirect rules can be obtained at once.
