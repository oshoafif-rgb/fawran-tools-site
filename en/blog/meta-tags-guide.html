You spent hours writing an excellent article, then published it, and after weeks noticed that the click-through rate from Google results is low even though the ranking is reasonable? The problem is usually not with the content itself, but with Title and description that the user sees before they even enter your page. In this guide, we will explain how to write Meta Tags professionally. Boosts your visitors' click-through rate from search results and social media posts, with a free code generator Ready to paste.

What are Meta Tags and why are they important?

Meta Tags are HTML tags placed inside the section <head> in your page, and not visible to the visitor Normal while browsing the page, but it is read from search engines and social media platforms to determine how Your page appears when it appears in search results or shares as a link. The top three types:

  • Title: The page title appears as a clickable blue link in Google results, and as the tab title in the browser.
  • Meta Description: The short description that appears under the title in search results.
  • Open Graph / Twitter Card: They control how the link preview appears when shared on Facebook, WhatsApp, LinkedIn, and Twitter.

How do you write an effective page title?

A good title balances two things: it naturally includes the keyword you are targeting, and at the same time, it is attractive enough to be worth clicking on among ten other results competing for the same interest. Practical tips:

  • Place the main keyword in the first 3-4 words of the title.
  • Keep the length between 50-60 characters so it doesn’t get cut off in search results.
  • Use numbers or a year (e.g., "2026") if appropriate — elements like these significantly increase the click-through rate.
  • Avoid repeating the same title on more than one page on your site — each page deserves a unique title that reflects its content.

How do you write a (Meta Description) that drives clicks?

The description is not a direct ranking factor in Google's algorithm, but it is your most powerful persuasion tool to turn page views into actual clicks. Think of it as a "free ad" two lines long. The best descriptions answer an implicit question in the searcher's mind: "Why should I enter this particular page?" Keep the length between 140-160 characters, and use a clear imperative verb (like "Learn," "Discover," "Try") at the beginning or end of the description.

Open Graph and Twitter Card: Why do you need them?

If someone shares your article link on WhatsApp or Facebook without having a proper Open Graph, the link will appear without an image or with a random image unrelated to the content — and this greatly reduces the likelihood of anyone clicking on it. Open Graph tags specify exactly: the title, description, and the image that will appear in the preview. Twitter Card is a similar tag but specifically for the X platform, and it is recommended to add both together to cover all sharing platforms.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the ideal length for a page title (Title Tag)?

Between about 50 and 60 characters. Google truncates longer titles in search results based on pixel width rather than the exact number of characters, so it is best to stay within this range to ensure it appears fully.

Do Meta Descriptions directly affect ranking on Google?

It does not directly affect the ranking algorithm, but it indirectly and very strongly affects click-through rate, and Google takes click-through rates and user behavior as a signal of page quality over time.

What is the difference between Open Graph and Twitter Card?

Open Graph is the standard used by Facebook, WhatsApp, LinkedIn, and most platforms to display link previews, while Twitter Card is a standard specific to the X platform only, so it is recommended to add both together.

Do I need a separate Open Graph image for each page?

This is preferable, especially for important articles, because a custom image related to the page content noticeably increases the click rate when shared, compared to a general, uniform image for the entire site.
