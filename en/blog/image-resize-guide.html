Did you upload an image to a certain platform and it came out oddly cropped or compressed in a way that distorts its appearance? The problem is usually that every social media platform or website has specific ideal image dimensions, and if your photo doesn’t match the required ratio, the platform is forced to crop or distort the image automatically. In this guide, we will explain how to resize your images accurately for any dimension you need, without losing important parts of the image or distorting its original proportions.

Why is the correct image size important for each platform?

Each platform (Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn, your online store) has an ideal aspect ratio for displaying images in the best possible way within its design. If you upload an image with a different ratio, the platform will either automatically crop part of it (which might be important!), add empty spaces around it, or in the worst case, stretch it and distort its appearance. Knowing the correct dimensions in advance and preparing the image accordingly saves you from all these surprises.

Fill modes: stretch, contain, and crop

Set extension (Cover): It fills the required size completely and may crop the edges of the image if the ratio is different. Suitable for background and cover images where details at the edges are not important.

Contain mode: It displays the full image without cutting any part, and adds margins (usually white or transparent) to fill the remaining space. Ideal for product images that need to appear fully without cutting any detail.

Custom crop: It allows you to select the exact part you want to appear from the original image before resizing, useful when the important element in the image is not exactly in the center.

Common mistakes that cause distorted images

The most common mistake is entering a width and height completely different from the original image ratio without enabling 'maintain aspect ratio' — the result is a stretched or unnaturally compressed image. Another mistake: enlarging a small image to a much larger size than the original, making it look blurry because enlargement does not add new details, only stretches existing pixels.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between resizing and cropping?

Resizing redraws the entire image with new dimensions, whereas cropping cuts a part of the original image without changing the scale of what remains.

Why does the image sometimes look distorted after resizing?

This happens when the width and height are changed with different ratios from the original image without activating the maintain aspect ratio option.

Does enlarging a small image improve its quality?

No, enlarging an image beyond its original size does not add new details that are not present; it just stretches the existing pixels, which may make it look blurry.

What’s the best fill setting for a product image on a white background?

The containment mode (Contain) is usually the most suitable, as it displays the full product without cutting any part of it.
