What is sitemap.xml and why your site needs it for Google

Article content:

  • What exactly is Sitemap.xml?
  • Why does it matter for Google indexing?
  • Basic components of the map file
  • How to create the map and submit it?
  • Frequently Asked Questions

If your website is new or recently launched, the search engine won't automatically know your pages from day one. The Sitemap.xml is like an official map you provide to Google yourself, showing all your important pages so it can discover and index them faster. In this guide, we will explain everything about it.

What exactly is Sitemap.xml?

A Sitemap.xml file is a text file in XML format that contains a list of all important links on your site, along with optional additional information about each link: last update date, frequency of content updates, and the page's priority relative to other pages on your site. This file does not appear to regular visitors — it is specifically directed at search engines.

Why does it matter for Google indexing?

Google discovers website pages in two main ways: following internal links from page to page, or relying on a sitemap submitted directly. The first method is slow and can miss isolated pages that are not sufficiently linked, especially on new or large websites. A sitemap completely solves this problem — it gives you direct control to inform Google about every important page you have, instead of relying on random discovery.

Basic components of the map file

  • loc: The full page link — the only required element.
  • lastmod: Date of last modification on the page content.
  • changefreq: Approximate update frequency (daily, weekly, monthly, etc.).
  • priority: A number from 0 to 1 indicates the relative importance of the page within your site.

How to create the map and submit it?

Using Sitemap generator on Fawran Tools:

  1. Paste your important site links, one link per line.
  2. Choose the appropriate default update frequency and priority for most of your pages.
  3. Click "Generate Sitemap" and get a ready, valid XML file.
  4. Download the file and upload it to the root of your site under the name sitemap.xml.
  5. Submit the link (yoursite.com/sitemap.xml) in the "Sitemaps" section within Google Search Console.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will my site never be indexed without a sitemap?

No, Google can discover your pages through internal links even without a sitemap, but Websitemap speeds up and ensures the discovery process.

How often should Websitemap be updated?

Every time you add or remove an important page from your site. Active sites with updated content should update their sitemap regularly.

What is the difference between priority and changefreq in Websitemap?

priority refers to the relative importance of the page within your site, while changefreq tells the search engine how often the content of that page is updated.

Where do I upload Websitemap.xml file on my site?

Usually uploaded to the root folder of your site, to be available at a link like yoursite.com/sitemap.xml.
