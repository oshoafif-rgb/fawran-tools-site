How to Find YouTube Keywords That Your Audience Actually Searches For

YouTube is the second largest search engine in the world after Google itself, but many content creators completely neglect the keyword research part, and just write a title that expresses the video from their own perspective — without verifying that these keywords are actually being searched by anyone. In this guide, we will explain how to discover the real search keywords that your target audience actually types in the search box, and how to use them intelligently.

Why are keywords important on YouTube?

YouTube’s algorithm relies heavily on understanding "what this video is about" in order to decide who to suggest it to. If your title, description, and tags match the words that people are really searching for, your chance increases that YouTube will display your video in direct search results, and in the "Recommended for you" section for viewers who watched similar content. The problem is that most content creators guess these keywords instead of verifying them with real data.

Where do the suggestions come from?

Tool YouTube keywords Fawran Tools uses the same autocomplete system that appears when you type in the search bar on YouTube itself — this means the words you see in the tool results are not guesses; they are actual search terms typed by other users. This gives you an accurate picture of your audience's real search intent, instead of relying solely on your intuition.

How to use the tool step by step?

  1. Write a video topic or keyword in the search box (for example, "Photoshop tutorial" or "cake recipe").
  2. Click "Extract Words" — a list of real suggestions from YouTube search will appear directly.
  3. Click on any word that suits your video to add it to the "Selected Words" box.
  4. Watch the character counter at the bottom of the box — YouTube allows a maximum of 500 characters in the Tags field.
  5. Copy the final list and paste it into the "Tags" field when uploading your video or editing an existing video.

When to use the expanded mode?

Regular search gives you immediate suggestions for your word, but sometimes you need a much wider list of ideas — especially if you are planning a full month's content in advance. In this case, activate the "expanded mode," which adds every Arabic letter after your main word and collects all resulting suggestions in a single unified list. The process takes a bit longer (since it performs about 28 searches instead of just one), but the result is worth it if you are planning seriously.

Where to put keywords in your video?

Keywords are not just for the Tags field. Best practice is to distribute them in four places:

  • Title: Place the most important word naturally, without excessive stuffing.
  • First two lines of the description: The part visible before "Show More" — the most important place in the entire description.
  • Tags field: 5-8 words actually related to the content.
  • Hashtags: 2-3 relevant hashtags in the description or title.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the ideal number of keywords for a single YouTube video?

Between 5 and 8 tags actually related to the video content are considered sufficient. YouTube allows a maximum of 500 characters in the Tags field, but quantity does not equal quality.

Are keywords more important than the title and description?

No, the title, description, and first chapters have a much greater impact on YouTube's understanding of video content and ranking. Tags are an additional supportive factor, not a replacement.

What is the difference between normal search and advanced mode in the tool?

Regular search gives you direct suggestions for only your entered word. Extended mode adds each Arabic letter after your word to discover additional, much broader suggestions.

Should I use the same keyword in all my videos?

No, it is preferable to vary the keywords according to the specific topic of each video, while keeping one or two words that represent your general channel identity if that is appropriate.
