How to protect registration forms from fake accounts using temporary emails.

Noticed many sign-ups on your site with strange emails that never respond? These are probably temporary email sites — services that give you a temporary inbox that works for just minutes, then disappears completely.

What are temporary email sites?

Sites like Mailinator, Guerrilla Mail, and YOPmail give you an instant random email address. You can receive an activation email or verification code without any registration or commitment. This inbox is temporary — it usually disappears after minutes or hours, and anyone who knows the address can see the same messages.

Why do people use it?

There are many legitimate uses: trying a service once without giving your real email, protecting against annoying newsletters later, or testing a registration form while developing a website. But it is also sometimes used to bypass the "one account per email" limit in free trials or first-time discount coupons.

What are the risks to your site or campaign?

If your site offers a free trial or discount coupon for first-time sign-ups, temporary emails open the door to unlimited exploitation of this offer. Also, if you are collecting a mailing list for marketing, temporary emails inflate the number of "subscribers" with fake numbers that do not represent a real audience who will interact with your messages in the future.

The list covers more than 100 common and well-known domains, but temporary email sites continuously appear with new domains, so no list can be 100% comprehensive. If your site is facing high-frequency fake registration attempts, you might need an additional layer of protection, such as phone number verification or bot detection systems (Captcha) alongside email checking.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the list cover all existing temporary email sites?

No, the list covers more than 100 common domains, but new sites keep appearing, so no list can be 100% comprehensive.

Is using a temporary email to register illegal?

No, it is not illegal in itself, but some websites consider it a violation of the Terms of Use, especially for exceeding a free subscription limit.

Can I integrate this check directly with my website's registration form?

The tool is designed for manual or batch checking. For direct programmatic integration, you will need a specialized API service for email verification.

Why do some people use a temporary email in the first place?

Many legitimate uses: trying a service once, protecting privacy from annoying newsletters, or testing registration forms during development.

Summary

Checking temporary emails is a simple step that protects the quality of your data and the integrity of your free offers from repeated abuse — it’s not a complete solution on its own, but it is an effective first line of defense against most fake registration attempts.
