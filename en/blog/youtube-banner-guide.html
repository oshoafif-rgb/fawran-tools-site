YouTube channel cover maker: correct size and safe zone

Have you noticed that some channel banners appear cut off or the text is incomplete on mobile? This problem is very common, and the reason is that YouTube displays the channel banner differently depending on the device — desktop, mobile, and TV each show a different part of the same image. In this guide, we will explain how to design your channel banner so it displays correctly on all devices without any surprises.

Why is the cover important for the channel's identity?

The channel cover is the first major visual impression any new visitor sees on your page, giving them a quick idea about your channel’s identity, the type of content you provide, and sometimes your posting schedule or social media links. A professional cover consistent with the rest of the channel’s identity (same colors and fonts used in the thumbnail and logo) builds immediate trust with the new visitor, whereas a default or randomly designed cover gives the impression of an unprofessional channel.

Official Size and Safe Zone

The full size for a YouTube channel cover is 2560×1440 pixelsBut the problem is that not all devices display this full size — each device crops a different part of the image depending on its screen shape. The portion guaranteed to appear on all devices (desktop, mobile, TV) is called the "safe area," and its size is… 1546×423 pixels Exactly in the middle of the image. Any important element (text, logo, face) should remain inside this area; the rest of the image can contain only non-critical background or decorations.

Practical design tips

Use the same color scheme used in your channel logo and thumbnails to build a consistent visual identity. Avoid padding Cover with lots of text — the name of the channel, maybe a simple logo or a short sentence is enough. If your channel has A fixed posting schedule (e.g. "New video every Thursday"), which you can add as a small sentence within the safe zone as information Extra useful for visitors.

Common mistakes that cause design cutting

The most common mistake is completely ignoring the safe area frame and relying only on what looks good in the full preview on desktop — then discovering that the text is cut off on mobile after publishing. A second mistake: placing text that is too long, extending horizontally beyond the safe area, especially with long channel names. The solution is always: review the design within the dotted frame only, and consider anything outside it as "optional decoration," not essential content.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does YouTube cut part of my channel cover on mobile?

Because the full cover is designed to fit all devices, but each device shows a different part of it. The safe area is the only part guaranteed to appear on all devices.

Should the text be only inside the safe area?

Yes, any important text or logo should remain within the safe area, while backgrounds and non-critical decorations can extend outside it.

What is the maximum file size for a channel cover?

YouTube sets a maximum size of 6 MB for the channel cover image, which is usually generous and does not affect reasonably exported images.

Can I use a photograph as a background instead of a color or gradient?

Yes, you can upload any image as a background, but make sure its important elements also fall within the safe area so they are not cut off on some devices.
