Do you know how long it takes for your homepage to fully appear for a new visitor? If the answer is "not sure," there's a good chance your site is losing visitors without you noticing — studies consistently confirm that every additional second of page load delay increases the percentage of visitors leaving before even seeing the content. In this guide, we will explain how to check your site's speed with Google's official tools, understand the results, and begin improving it effectively.

Why is your site speed really important?

Speed is not just a better user experience — it is also an official ranking factor announced by Google itself within the search algorithm. That means two websites with almost the same content quality, the faster one is usually ranked higher in search results. Additionally, slow websites lose real sales if they are e-commerce stores — industry e-commerce numbers consistently show a direct relationship between site slowness and a lower purchase completion rate.

A simplified explanation of Core Web Vitals metrics

Google measures speed through a set of metrics called Core Web Vitals, the most important of which are three:

  • LCP (Largest Contentful Paint): Time until the largest visible element appears on the page (usually the cover image or main headline). Goal: less than 2.5 seconds.
  • CLS (Layout Stability): It measures the extent of 'jumping' of page elements during loading (like when an image loads after the text and shifts it). Goal: less than 0.1.
  • TBT (Total Block Time): The time when the browser is busy processing JavaScript code and cannot respond to user interaction. Goal: less than 200 milliseconds.

Fastest Steps to Improve Performance Scores

Most speed issues on small and medium websites are due to one common reason: Large images uncompressedIf you have images of several megabytes uploaded directly from the camera, this is the fastest place to start from. Try it. Compress your images And convert it to the format WebP — These two steps alone can noticeably improve LCP scores on most websites, without any complicated programming changes.

Why is the mobile result always lower?

Have you noticed that mobile scores are always lower than desktop scores for the same site? This is normal and intentional — Google simulates a slower network and weaker processor when testing mobile, as this reflects the real reality for a large portion of visitors browsing from mobile or lower-powered devices. For this reason, mobile scores are actually the most important metric that you should focus on improving first.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is LCP and how do I improve it?

LCP measures the time until the largest visible element on the page appears. To improve it: compress images, use the WebP format, and reduce the size of CSS and JS files loaded before the content appears.

Why is the mobile result usually lower than the computer result?

Mobile testing simulates a slower network and weaker processor on purpose, because this represents the actual reality for a large portion of visitors, which makes the mobile result always stricter.

Do low performance scores affect my site’s ranking on Google?

Yes, Core Web Vitals is an officially announced ranking factor by Google, especially for websites with similar content quality.

Can I improve the speed of my site without programming knowledge?

Partly yes — compressing images before uploading and converting them to WebP format is a step anyone can take without technical expertise, and it significantly improves speed.
